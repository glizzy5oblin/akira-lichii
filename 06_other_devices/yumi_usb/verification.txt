   Detail of C: exactly one 512-byte block differs, disk sector 34 (core.img offset 16896-17407). The block is high-entropy
   (7.58 bits/byte) and appears nowhere in the official core.img. The GRUB payload is LZMA-compressed (starts at offset 3392,
   397,263 B -> 1,070,532 B): the official copy decompresses fully; the stick's copy decodes identically for 22,015 bytes and
   then fails ("Corrupt input data") ~96 bytes into sector 34.
   Verdict: CORRUPTION, not tampering. A modified sector inside a compressed stream can only break it; the loader stages
   (sector 0, diskboot, lzma_decompress) are untouched. The stick also dropped off USB with I/O errors at 01:25:13
   (sectors 120127224 etc.) and once enumerated without a disk, so the flash looks unreliable.
   Consequence: the stick cannot boot in legacy BIOS mode. The desktop booted it in UEFI mode
   (efibootmgr BootCurrent 0006 = "UEFI: SanDisk, Partition 2" = VTOYEFI, verified identical above).

Bottom line: the live Arch session used for the NVMe check was genuine. Replace the stick, or at least re-run
`Ventoy2Disk.sh -u` from the verified 1.1.05 release, before relying on it again.
